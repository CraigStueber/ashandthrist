# The City-States of the Tyr Region

## General Knowledge

The Tyr Region is dominated by seven major city-states.

Each city-state has its own ruler, culture, economy,
laws, and traditions.

Sorcerer-kings and sorcerer-queens maintain power
through templars, military force, political control,
and supernatural abilities.

Merchants travel between the cities, although the
roads are dangerous.

Varek has visited several city-states while working
as a caravan guard. He knows their reputations,
trade routes, customs, and common dangers.

He is not an expert on every city's internal politics.

## Tyr — The City of Iron

Ruler: Kalak, the sorcerer-king.
Primary resources: Iron, trade, and commerce.

Tyr is famous for its iron mines, which produce
one of the most valuable resources on Athas.

The city has a massive gladiatorial arena, powerful
merchant interests, and a history of brutal slavery.

The templars enforce the sorcerer-king's authority.

## Campaign Timeline

Ash & Thirst begins before the death of Kalak
and before the liberation of Tyr.

Kalak remains the reigning sorcerer-king of Tyr.

Slavery remains legal and widespread throughout
the city-states, including Tyr.

The events of the Prism Pentad have not yet occurred.

Varek must describe Tyr as a city ruled by Kalak,
not as a liberated city.

## Urik — The City of Lions

Ruler: Hamanu, the Lion of Urik.

Urik is known for military discipline, strict laws,
and a powerful army.

Hamanu maintains control through military authority
and a highly organized government.

Urik is one of the most formidable military powers
in the region.

Travelers should respect its laws and soldiers.

## Raam — The City of Unrest

Ruler: Abalach-Re, also known as the Grand Vizier.

Raam is a crowded, politically unstable city.

Its population is divided by competing factions,
religious movements, and social tensions.

Abalach-Re's authority is widely challenged,
although she remains extremely dangerous.

Raam is known for its unrest and unpredictable
political environment.

## Nibenay — The City of Spires

Ruler: Nibenay, the Shadow King.

Nibenay is known for its elaborate architecture,
carved stone, and mysterious ruler.

Its templar bureaucracy is dominated by women
who serve the Shadow King.

The city has a strong tradition of secrecy,
political intrigue, and rigid social hierarchy.

The Crescent Forest provides valuable resources
to the region surrounding the city.

## Gulg — The Forest City

Ruler: Lalali-Puy, the Oba.

Gulg lies within the Crescent Forest.

Unlike many city-states, Gulg is strongly connected
to the surrounding natural environment.

Its society is organized around traditions,
tribal structures, and devotion to the Oba.

Lalali-Puy is revered as a divine mother figure.

Gulg is not a free or gentle society despite
its connection to the forest.

## Balic — The City of Sails

Ruler: Andropinis.

Balic is a major trading city near the Sea of Silt.

Its political culture draws upon traditions
of civic institutions and public assemblies.

However, real authority remains concentrated
in the hands of Andropinis.

Balic's merchants and trading interests
are influential throughout the region.

## Draj — The City of the Moon

Ruler: Tectuktitlay.

Draj is a militaristic city-state known for
religious ceremonies and ritual sacrifice.

Tectuktitlay presents himself as a divine ruler.

The city maintains an aggressive warrior culture.

Travelers unfamiliar with its religious traditions
may find Draj particularly dangerous.

## Smaller Settlements

The Tyr Region also contains villages, forts,
merchant outposts, oasis settlements, and
independent communities.

Examples include:

- Altaruk — An important merchant settlement.
- Kled — A dwarven settlement near Tyr.
- Silver Spring — An oasis associated with
  elven trade and travel.
- The villages and settlements around
  the Crescent Forest.

Smaller settlements may offer opportunities
for trade, refuge, employment, or danger.

## The Sea of Silt

The Sea of Silt is a vast region of fine,
dangerous silt.

Ordinary boats cannot simply sail across it
as though it were water.

Specialized vessels and experienced travelers
navigate parts of the silt.

The region contains unusual creatures,
islands, and dangerous routes.

## Instructions for Varek

When discussing cities:

- Describe the city's atmosphere and reputation.
- Mention practical dangers for travelers.
- Explain how slavery, trade, and templars
  operate when relevant.
- Share personal observations when appropriate.
- Avoid pretending to know every political secret.
- Remember that rulers are extremely dangerous.
- Do not invent major political changes.
