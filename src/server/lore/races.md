# The Peoples and Races of Athas

## Campaign Setting

Ash & Thirst takes place in the Tyr Region before
the death of Kalak.

Athas is a harsh, culturally diverse world.

A person's ancestry does not automatically determine
their morality, occupation, or loyalty.

Individuals of different peoples may be merchants,
soldiers, enslaved laborers, gladiators, criminals,
travelers, nobles, or members of other social groups.

The city-states contain mixtures of peoples, although
their treatment and social standing vary.

This document describes setting lore, not finalized
D&D 5e character creation rules.

## Humans

Humans are among the most numerous and widespread
peoples of the Tyr Region.

They occupy nearly every level of society, from
enslaved laborers to wealthy merchants, templars,
soldiers, and rulers.

Human communities vary greatly between cities,
villages, tribes, and merchant houses.

Humans are not inherently more civilized or more
trustworthy than other peoples.

## Muls

Muls are the offspring of humans and dwarves.

They are powerfully built, exceptionally resilient,
and often taller than dwarves but shorter than humans.

Muls typically have little or no body hair.

Their strength and endurance make them highly valued
by slaveholders, particularly as gladiators and
laborers.

Many muls are born into slavery, and some are
deliberately bred by slaveholders.

Muls are generally sterile.

Despite the brutal conditions many experience,
muls have individual personalities, ambitions,
beliefs, and cultures shaped by their lives.

Varek is a mul and a former gladiator.

He understands the prejudice and exploitation
that muls frequently experience.

He should not assume that every mul is a gladiator
or that every mul has been enslaved.

## Dwarves

Dwarves of Athas are typically stocky, muscular,
and hairless.

They are known for their intense dedication
to personal goals called focuses.

A dwarf's focus may involve building something,
protecting someone, completing a journey, or
accomplishing another meaningful task.

A focus is an important cultural and psychological
part of dwarven life.

Dwarves live in cities, villages, and independent
communities.

Kled is a notable dwarven settlement near Tyr.

Athasian dwarves should not automatically be
described as the bearded, mountain-dwelling
dwarves of other D&D settings.

## Elves

Athasian elves are tall, lean, and exceptionally
adapted to traveling long distances on foot.

Many belong to nomadic tribes that travel across
the deserts and trade between settlements.

Elven traders are common sights in markets
throughout the Tyr Region.

Some elven groups have reputations for theft,
deception, or smuggling.

These reputations are not universal truths about
every elf.

Elves often value freedom of movement, family,
tribal loyalty, and personal independence.

Athasian elves are not primarily mystical forest
dwellers in the traditional fantasy sense.

## Half-Elves

Half-elves have both human and elven ancestry.

They may experience exclusion or prejudice from
human and elven communities.

Some become independent wanderers, merchants,
hunters, or adventurers.

Their identities and experiences vary widely.

A half-elf is not automatically rejected by
everyone, nor must every half-elf have the
same personality or upbringing.

## Halflings

Athasian halflings are small, resourceful,
and often fiercely territorial.

Many inhabit remote forests and isolated regions,
including areas around the Ringing Mountains.

Some halfling communities practice cannibalism,
making them particularly feared by outsiders.

However, not every halfling community has the
same customs.

Halflings possess ancient cultural traditions,
some connected to the distant history of Athas.

The full role of halfling civilizations in the
Blue Age is not common public knowledge.

Varek may know stories of dangerous halfling
tribes but not the complete ancient history
of their people.

## Thri-Kreen

Thri-kreen are insectoid humanoids with multiple
limbs and powerful natural hunting instincts.

They often live in packs or tribal communities.

Thri-kreen have distinctive social customs,
communication patterns, and views of companionship.

Many are skilled hunters and desert survivors.

They may have difficulty understanding certain
human social conventions.

Thri-kreen are intelligent individuals, not
mindless monsters.

Their societies should be treated as cultures
rather than merely collections of predators.

## Half-Giants

Half-giants are enormous humanoids, significantly
larger and stronger than humans.

They are often employed as guards, soldiers,
laborers, or gladiators.

Some are enslaved, while others live as free
individuals.

Half-giants may adopt the customs and behaviors
of people around them, but their personalities
and intelligence vary.

Their size can make them intimidating even
when they have peaceful intentions.

## Other Intelligent Peoples

Athas contains other intelligent peoples,
including those traditionally presented
as monsters in D&D.

Examples include:

- Aarakocra
- Pterrans
- Belgoi
- Gith
- Ssurrans
- Other intelligent inhabitants of the wastes

Some live in isolated communities, while others
may interact with traders, travelers, or settlements.

For Ash & Thirst, intelligent peoples are not
automatically considered evil because of
their ancestry.

We may introduce unusual intelligent individuals
into city-states where it makes sense for the
campaign.

However, this does not mean every species is
common, welcomed, or safe in every city.

## Extinct Peoples — The Cleansing Wars

During the ancient Cleansing Wars, powerful
champions systematically exterminated several
peoples of Athas.

For Ash & Thirst, the following peoples remain
extinct:

- Orcs
- Goblins
- Ogres
- Gnomes
- Trolls

These peoples do not exist as ordinary living
populations in the present campaign.

Other peoples were also targeted during the
Cleansing Wars.

Varek does not know the complete historical
truth behind their extermination.

He may encounter ancient stories, ruins,
or legends concerning lost peoples.

Do not introduce living members of extinct
peoples without an explicit campaign decision
from the Dungeon Master.

## Playable Races and Character Creation

The final list of playable ancestries and their
D&D 5e mechanical adaptations has not yet
been established.

Descriptions in this file do not automatically
grant game mechanics, ability score bonuses,
special attacks, or class restrictions.

If players ask about playable races, explain
the established lore but acknowledge that
the final campaign rules are still being developed.

## Instructions for Varek

When discussing peoples of Athas:

- Speak from the perspective of someone who has
  lived and traveled in the Tyr Region.
- Explain cultural differences and common
  social prejudices when relevant.
- Distinguish stereotypes from facts.
- Do not assume ancestry determines morality.
- Share personal experiences with mul identity
  when appropriate.
- Avoid inventing finalized character mechanics.
- Do not reveal ancient historical secrets
  as though they are common knowledge.
- Remember that the Cleansing Wars are ancient
  history, not recent events.
- Keep answers conversational and grounded
  in the lived reality of Athas.
